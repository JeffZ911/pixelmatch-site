# Amazon Vine Reviewer Image Requirements and Best Practices (2026)

To enroll in Vine, sellers need at least one compliant image (minimum 1000px, pure white background). For Vine reviewers, uploaded photos must exclude barcodes, shipping packaging, and medical claims to avoid automated review rejection.

## Detailed Requirements

![Detailed Requirements](/img/amazon-vine-reviewer-image-requirements-best-practices/inline-2.webp)


Enroll your product in Vine only after confirming your listing meets the baseline eligibility criteria. Amazon requires that the product is [FBA-only (Fulfillment by Amazon)](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G201114120), has [fewer than 30 reviews](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G201114120) already published, and possesses a "New" condition status. If your images do not meet the technical standards below, the Vine dashboard will return an "Action Required" status, preventing you from distributing units to reviewers.

### Seller Image Requirements for Vine Enrollment

Your main image acts as the gatekeeper for the Vine program. Amazon's automated "Imaging Service" scans every upload to ensure it follows the strict [Product Image Requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G1881). 

1.  **The 85% Rule:** The product must fill at least 85% of the frame. If you leave too much white space around the item, the image will be flagged. This is particularly important for small items like jewelry or electronics components.
2.  **Pure White Background:** Your background must be exactly [RGB 255, 255, 255](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G1881). Off-white or light grey backgrounds (common in "raw" smartphone photos) will result in enrollment rejection.
3.  **Resolution for Zoom:** While the absolute minimum is 500 pixels, you must hit at least [1000 pixels on the longest side](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G1881) to enable the "hover-to-zoom" feature. High-performing listings in 2026 generally utilize [2000 x 2000 pixels](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G1881) to maintain clarity on 4K mobile screens.
4.  **No Prohibited Elements:** Your main image cannot contain text, logos, watermarks, or inset images. It must show the actual product only — no "lifestyle" props are allowed in the primary slot.

### Guidelines for Vine Reviewer Photos

Vine Voices (the reviewers) are elite contributors, but they are still subject to the same [Community Guidelines](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GLHXEX85WAMZFEHL) as any other customer. While Amazon encourages reviewers to upload media to provide a "comprehensive" look at the product, it is not a mandatory requirement for them to keep their Vine status.

Reviewers are instructed to focus their photos on the product's performance and physical attributes. If a reviewer includes a photo of the "shipping experience" (e.g., a crushed brown Amazon box), that specific photo — and often the entire review — may be rejected during the moderation phase. This is because Amazon considers shipping issues to be the fault of the carrier or the fulfillment center, not the product itself.

### Why Vine Reviews Get Blocked

1.  **Visible Barcodes and QR Codes:** This is the most common technical reason for rejection. If a reviewer takes a photo of the back of your box to show the ingredients or instructions, and a [UPC or FNSKU barcode](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GLHXEX85WAMZFEHL) is visible, the bot will likely flag it for containing "identifiable information" or "promotional links."
2.  **Medical and Health Claims:** In the United States, Amazon is extremely aggressive regarding [FDA compliance](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G200164490). If your product packaging includes claims like "cures insomnia" or "treats anxiety," and a reviewer’s photo clearly shows that text, the image will be suppressed.
3.  **Personal Information:** Photos that accidentally capture a reviewer’s shipping label (showing their address), a license plate in the background, or a child's face are [routinely blocked](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GLHXEX85WAMZFEHL) to protect privacy.
4.  **External Links:** If your packaging prominently displays your website URL or a "Join our VIP club" insert, and the reviewer captures this in a photo, it violates the policy against [directing customers away from Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=GLHXEX85WAMZFEHL).

### Why Seller Images Fail Enrollment

If you cannot even get your product into the Vine dashboard, check your main image for these "silent killers":
*   **The "Shadow" Problem:** If your product has a heavy, non-natural drop shadow or is floating in a way that looks clearly "photoshopped," the automated audit may fail.
*   **Graphic Overlays:** Adding a "Top Rated" or "BPA Free" badge to your main image is a [violation of the Main Image policy](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/G1881).
*   **Multiple Views:** Showing three different angles of the product in the *first* image slot is prohibited. The main image must show only one product (or one set if sold as a bundle).

### Packaging Tweaks for Reviewers

Standardize your packaging design to be "reviewer-friendly." Since you cannot control how a Vine Voice takes a photo, you must control what is available for them to photograph.

*   **Move Barcodes to the Bottom:** Place your FNSKU or UPC barcode on the bottom panel of your packaging. Reviewers rarely photograph the bottom of a box; they focus on the front, back, and sides. This simple move prevents the "barcode rejection" bot from triggering.
*   **Use Matte Packaging:** High-gloss boxes create "hot spots" (glare) when reviewers use a camera flash. This glare can obscure product details and lead to low-quality images. Matte finishes photograph better in home environments.
*   **Isolate Claims:** If you must include specific health or technical claims, place them on a separate "Instructions for Use" sheet inside the box rather than on the external packaging. This keeps the claims out of the "lifestyle" photos reviewers typically take.
