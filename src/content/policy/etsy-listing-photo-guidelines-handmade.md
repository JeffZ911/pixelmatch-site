## Detailed Requirements

![Detailed Requirements](/img/etsy-listing-photo-guidelines-handmade/inline-2.webp)


Etsy's 2026 requirements are designed to create a uniform browsing experience while protecting the integrity of the "Handmade" category. While the technical side focuses on pixels and bits, the policy side focuses on transparency and consumer trust.

### Handmade Policy Rules

The most critical rule for 2026 is the **Originality Requirement**. To qualify as a handmade item, your primary listing photo must be a real photograph of the actual product you (or your shop members) created. 

1.  **No Stock Photos for Primary Images:** You cannot use a manufacturer's stock photo or a generic 3D rendering as your first image. This is a common point of failure for sellers who buy components (like blank t-shirts or jewelry settings) and use the supplier's professional photos.
2.  **The "Finished Item" Rule:** For custom-made items, your first photo must show a finished example of your work. You are permitted to use computer-generated mockups for secondary images to show color variations or personalization options, but the thumbnail that appears in search must be a physical product.
3.  **Proving Craftsmanship:** undisclosed regarding the exact percentage of listings flagged by Etsy’s new AI-detection tool for "non-original content," but seller reports in early 2026 suggest that listings with "too-perfect" AI backgrounds on the *primary* image are receiving manual reviews more frequently than in 2025.

### Format Restrictions

While Etsy supports five file types, they are not treated equally by the platform's processing engine.

*   **.jpg / .jpeg:** This remains the gold standard. It offers the best balance of file size and detail.
*   **.png:** High quality, but often exceeds the 1MB limit. **Crucial Rule:** Etsy does not support transparency. If you upload a transparent .png (a product with no background), Etsy’s system will automatically fill the transparent areas with solid black. This is a "glitch" that ruins the aesthetic of hundreds of shops daily.
*   **.heic:** This is the default format for iPhones. While Etsy supports it, the conversion process can sometimes lead to color shifting. For the most accurate color representation, convert .heic to .jpg (sRGB) before uploading.
*   **.gif:** While supported, **animated GIFs are strictly prohibited**. Only static .gif files will work. If you want to show movement, use the Etsy Listing Video slot (max 15 seconds, no audio).

**Actionable Step:** Set your camera or smartphone to "Most Compatible" (JPEG) rather than "High Efficiency" (HEIC) in your settings menu to ensure the colors you see on your phone match what the buyer sees on the site.

If you see the dreaded red banner "There was an error uploading your image," it is rarely a server-side Etsy problem. It is almost always a file-side configuration error.

### Upload Timeouts and File Size

The 1MB limit is a hard cap for a reason. Etsy’s mobile app (the primary tool for many sellers) struggles to process 5MB or 10MB "straight-out-of-the-camera" files. When the upload takes too long, the connection resets, and you get a generic error message. High-resolution images from modern DSLRs or iPhones often land between 4MB and 8MB. These must be compressed before they touch the Etsy uploader.

### The Black Background Glitch

As mentioned, the "Black Background" issue occurs when a seller uses a background removal tool but saves the file as a transparent PNG. Etsy’s image processor cannot render "nothingness." It defaults to black. This is particularly damaging for sellers in the wedding or jewelry niches, where a clean white or lifestyle background is expected.

### Broken Zoom and Search Suppression

If your image is too small (under 1000px), Etsy will still allow the upload, but it will flag the listing internally. Smaller images are harder to see on high-density mobile screens (Retina displays). Consequently, Etsy's search algorithm may prioritize listings with high-resolution photos that allow for a high-quality zoom experience. If you notice your traffic dropping despite having a great product, check your pixel dimensions.

**Actionable Step:** Inspect your "Listing [Quality Score](https://pixelmatch.art/blog/policy/etsy-search-quality-score-image-requirements-2026)" in your shop dashboard. If Etsy flags "[Image Quality](https://pixelmatch.art/blog/compare/tiktok-shop-mall-invitation-image-quality-fix)" as a suggestion, it is a direct signal that your resolution or aspect ratio is hindering your **ranking lower in search**.

### Compressing Large Files

To fix the 1MB timeout issue, use a compression tool that maintains the 2000px dimension. 
*   **Adobe Express:** Etsy currently offers a 3-month premium trial for Adobe Express to many sellers (check your "Shop Manager" dashboard for the link). This tool allows for quick resizing and compression.
*   **Canva:** Use the "Resize" tool to ensure your shortest side is 2000px, then export as a JPG with the quality slider set to 80%. This usually brings a 5MB file down to roughly 600KB without visible loss in quality.
*   **Squoosh.app:** A free, Google-backed tool that allows you to see a side-by-side comparison of your image as you compress it.

### Fixing Blurry Zoom

If your photos are blurry when zoomed, you are likely "upscaling" small images. You cannot take a 500px image and simply change the settings to 2000px; this just creates a pixelated mess. 
1.  Go back to your original source file (the photo exactly as it was taken).
2.  Ensure you are not cropping too tightly. If you crop 80% of a photo away, you lose 80% of the resolution.
3.  Export at 2000px. 
4.  undisclosed regarding Etsy's exact internal compression algorithm updates for 2026, but current testing suggests that images exported at 72 DPI (dots per inch) vs 300 DPI show no visible difference on the platform, as Etsy converts all uploads to 72 DPI for web display. Focus on the pixel count, not the DPI.

### Fixing Handmade Policy Violations

If your listing was deactivated for a "Handmade Policy" violation related to photos, you must replace the primary image with a "raw" photo. 
*   **The Hero Shot:** Take a photo of the product on a neutral, physical background (like a wooden table or a plain piece of cardstock). This proves the item exists.
*   **The Secondary Shots:** Use tools like Photoroom, Pebblely, or PhotoAI to create lifestyle scenes for your 2nd through 10th images. These tools are excellent for showing your handmade ceramic mug on a cozy morning coffee table or your handmade jewelry on a model. 
*   **The Verification Shot:** Many sellers are now including a "process" photo as their 10th image—a shot of the item half-finished on a workbench. This is a powerful deterrent against AI-detection flags.

**Actionable Step:** Create a "Master Export Preset" in your editing software. Set it to: JPG, sRGB, 2000px on the shortest side, 80% quality. Run every photo through this preset before uploading to ensure 100% consistency across your shop.
