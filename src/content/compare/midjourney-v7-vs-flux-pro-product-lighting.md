## Where Each Falls Short

![Where Each Falls Short](/img/midjourney-v7-vs-flux-pro-product-lighting/inline-5.webp)


Neither model is a "magic button" without understanding their limitations.

[Midjourney v7](https://pixelmatch.art/blog/compare/midjourney-v7-for-amazon-product-photography-20260704) struggles with literalism. Its tendency to add heavy stylization can misrepresent product materials—for example, turning a matte plastic finish into a glossy one because it "looks better" to the AI. This leads to potential marketplace returns if the item looks different in person. Furthermore, Midjourney lacks a native API, meaning you cannot easily plug it into your inventory management system to batch-generate marketplace-compliant product images. Every image must be manually prompted and downloaded.

Flux Pro can sometimes feel too "raw" or neutral without heavy prompt engineering. If you aren't specific, the lighting can look like a basic fluorescent office bulb. It lacks Midjourney's built-in style reference simplicity for instant artistic flair, requiring more technical setup or a dedicated SaaS tool to get the best results.

Both models require a learning curve if used natively, which is why many sellers abandon raw prompting in favor of purpose-built tools like Photoroom, which offers a Pro tier at $12.99/month for basic background removal, or PixelMatch for full-scale AI scene generation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Midjourney v7 follow Amazon's white background rules?
Midjourney can generate white backgrounds using the `--no props` parameter or specific "studio lighting" prompts, but it often adds subtle shadows or gradients that may trigger Amazon's automated suppression filters. For [Amazon FBA main image requirements](https://sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub/reference/external/201102660), a dedicated background removal tool or a Flux-based engine is more reliable for achieving the required Hex code #FFFFFF.

### Which model is better for clothing and apparel?
Flux Pro generally performs better for apparel because it respects fabric textures (like the weave of linen or the sheen of silk) more accurately than Midjourney. Midjourney tends to "beautify" clothes, sometimes adding folds or drapes that don't exist on the actual garment, which can mislead customers.

### Can I use my own product photos with these models?
Yes, but the workflow differs. In Midjourney, you use "Image Prompts" or "Character References" (modified for objects), which can be inconsistent. Flux Pro is typically used in a "LoRA" (Low-Rank Adaptation) workflow or through a SaaS like PixelMatch, which allows you to upload a photo of your actual product and "re-light" it in a new environment while keeping the product's geometry 100% intact.
